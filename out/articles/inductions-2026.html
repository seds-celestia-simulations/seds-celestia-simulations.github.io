September 3, 2026

By SEDS Celestia Simulations

So interviews are done. If you're reading this you made it past that stage, and the next three weeks are where it stops being about talking about simulations and starts being about building them.

How it works

  • Inductions are assignment based, three weeks, three assignments.
  • Weekly workshops will be organised to teach you everything you need to know for that week's announcement. Times go up on our Instagram as well as the Simulations Induction Whatsapp group.
  • Week 1 is the same for everyone.
  • From Week 2 you pick a track, ML or Ray Tracing, and stay on it through Week 3.
  • Each assignment has a spec PDF and a code template in the repo, and asks you to write up two or more questions alongside the code.
  • Everything gets submitted as a GitHub link. No zips, no email attachments.
  • Don't worry about the details, we will explain everything in the workshops.

Week 1: Rigid Balls, Round Walls

Everyone starts in the same place: gravity integration and collision detection for rigid balls bouncing around inside a circular boundary, rendered with Pygame. It's a small, self-contained physics sim. Nothing flashy, the point is to get your hands dirty with integrators, collision response, and the kind of numerical edge cases every simulation eventually runs into.

Weeks 2 and 3: To Train or to Trace?

This is where the paths split.

Track A: Machine Learning

Week 2 has you building a grid-based simulation from scratch. Week 3 turns around and uses that same simulation as a data source: you train a model on the data it generates and it learns to predict the system's behavior. If the idea of your own simulation becoming the training set appeals, this is the one for you.

Track B: Ray Tracing

Week 2 keeps things simple. Ray tracing in 2D, enough to get the geometry and the intuition right. Week 3 is where it gets real: you extend that into a full, proper ray tracer. If tracing light sounds better than training models, pick this.

Which means each week we're looking for two things:

  • The TODO blocks filled in, however far you got.
  • A short writeup in that week's README.md, answers to the questions in the spec, plus what you make of your own results: where it broke, what surprised you, what you couldn't get working. Half a page is plenty.

How to submit

The repo's README has the full step-by-step, including the exact commands and the Windows-specific notes. Short version:

  1. Use the "Use this template" button on the repo to create your own public copy under your account, then clone it.
  2. Do the work, write up your README, and push.
  3. Drop your repo link in that week's form.
